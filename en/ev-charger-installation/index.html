Good to know

Before you install a charger

  • Dedicated circuitA charger gets its own circuit in the meter cupboard, so the rest of your home keeps working.
  • Single or three-phaseA three-phase connection charges considerably faster than single-phase.
  • Load balancingWith load balancing the charger adapts to your usage, so your main connection is not overloaded.
  • Own propertyWe install chargers on a driveway, carport or in a garage. For a charger on the street, go through your municipality.
  • Distance mattersThe further the charger is from the meter cupboard, the more cable is needed.
  • Fuse box checkSometimes the fuse box needs to be upgraded or extended first.

FAQ

EV charger questions

Do I need single-phase or three-phase?

Single-phase charges up to about 3.7 kW, three-phase up to about 11 kW. What makes sense depends on your car and how much you drive. A photo of your meter cupboard shows us what you have now.

Does my fuse box need upgrading?

That depends on your current board. There has to be room for a dedicated charger circuit. If there isn't, we extend or replace the board.

Can I get a charger on the street?

A charger on the public road is arranged through your municipality. We install chargers on your own property, such as a driveway, carport or garage.

Can I charge with my solar panels?

Many smart chargers can match their charging current to your solar output. We are happy to discuss what is possible in your situation.
